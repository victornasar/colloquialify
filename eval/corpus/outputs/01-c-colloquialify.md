I keep seeing teams treat coding assistants like junior engineers. They aren't.

They help on the easy 40%: config, test scaffolding, repetitive refactors. Review time often goes up anyway, because people stop reading the boring parts carefully. The failures I care about aren't exotic. They're the ones you catch when you still own the diff.

If the tool writes the easy part, you still own 100% of the merge. That trade only works if review standards stay annoying. Acceptance rate is not correctness, and correctness is not value.

I don't need a ban. I need the same bar I use for docs and PRs: say what changed, verify it, and don't ship claims you didn't check.
