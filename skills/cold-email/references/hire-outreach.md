# Hire outreach (recruiter / hiring manager)

Use when the goal is a **job reply**, not problem discovery.
Triggers: cold email a recruiter or hiring manager, intro for an open role,
“message this person about a job,” or when the user points at this framework.

This is **not** the discovery middle path. Do not invent an HVAC-style intrigue
question. Do not use open-hiring-dm’s ultra-short “want in / resume?” shape
unless the recipient already posted that applications or DMs are open (then
prefer colloquialify’s open-hiring-dm).

## Expectations

Cold hire reply rates stay low. This framework raises the floor by forcing
diligence and relevance and by cutting the moves that kill most emails. It is
not magic.

- **Hiring managers** (startup founders, eng leads): highest upside. Specific
  diligence + one real insight lands hardest.
- **Volume Talent Acquisition / corporate recruiters:** still worth sending.
  Many will reply “please apply through the portal.” Calibrate for that. Keep
  the note professional; do not complain about ATS.
- **Internal referral path:** shorter. Who you are + role + ask them to put
  your name in. Links optional if they already know your work.

## Framework (keep the beats; cut fluff inside each)

Short and sweet:

1. **Who you are** — one plain line (role + stack or focus).
2. **Why you’re emailing ME** — proof of diligence. Name their post, team,
   product, or a specific detail that shows this isn’t a blast. Specific
   detail beats “I saw your posting.”
3. **What you’re looking for** — proof of relevance. The role or lane, in
   ordinary words (no “req”).
4. **Link to CV / resume** — proof of excellence.
5. **Link to portfolio** — proof of creativity (site, GitHub, shipped work).
6. **Unique insight** — proof of value. One concrete observation about their
   product, team, or problem. Not mission parroting. Not “I’m passionate.”
   **Omit if you don’t have a real one.** A forced or generic insight is worse
   than none. Short corporate TA notes often skip this beat on purpose.

## Shape

- **Subject** = role + light specificity (e.g. `Growth full-stack application`,
  `frontend at TypeSafe`). Intent over cleverness.
- **Body** = the beats in order, tight paragraphs or short lines. Blank line
  before links so they breathe on mobile.
- **Audience mode** still applies: skeptic (lowercase) vs trade (normal caps).
  Muslim recipients: salaam ± short dua first when voice profile says so.
- **Corporate recruiter email (default when emailing Talent Acquisition /
  big-company recruiters):** professional normal caps. `Hi {First},` is OK.
  Short `Thank you,` + full name sign-off is OK. Stay direct and polished.
  Do **not** sound like a shortcut around their process.
- **Startup / hiring-manager email:** can be shorter and more human (skeptic
  lowercase OK when that matches voice). Still no call ask. Prefer a real
  product/team detail over polished filler.
- **No em dashes.**
- **No call asks.** Resume + portfolio are the proof; routing ask is fine.
- **Length:** hire mail can go past 240 chars because of links. Still cut
  first. If it needs a second screen, you wrote too much.

### Corporate recruiter example (shape only — do not parrot)

```
Hi Philura,

I came across the Software Engineer II (remote) role and wanted to reach out
directly.

I'm a full-stack engineer (React, TypeScript, Node, Postgres) with experience
owning production applications end to end. I'd welcome the chance to contribute
to the team.

Portfolio: https://www.nasarissa.com
GitHub: https://github.com/victornasar

I've attached my resume. Would you be able to route this to the appropriate
hiring manager?

Thank you,
Nasar Issa
```

### Startup / hiring-manager example (shape only — do not parrot)

```
I'm a full-stack engineer. I'm emailing because you hire for [team/role], and I
want the [exact role].

Resume: https://nasarissa.com
Work: https://github.com/victornasar

[One concrete line about their product or problem that a name-swap would break.]
```

## Cut

- Mission / passion / “join the frontier” fluff
- Recruiter jargon the writer wouldn’t say (“req,” “loop,” “top of funnel”)
- Soft chat asks (“happy to hop on a call,” “would love to connect”)
- Generic insight that could fit any company after a noun swap (omit instead)
- Second unique-insight paragraph (one is enough)
- **ATS / Workday complaints** as the reason for emailing (“the form is long,”
  “application is lengthy,” “wanted to skip the website”). Reads as a shortcut
  and disrespect for their process. Always cut. Reach out because of the role
  and fit, not because applying is inconvenient.
