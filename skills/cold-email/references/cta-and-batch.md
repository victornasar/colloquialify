# CTA and batch gate

## CTA

- **Hard ban** call asks and the whole family: “15 min,” “quick call,” “hop on
  a chat,” “would love to chat,” “open to hopping on something,” “grab time on
  my cal,” etc.
- CTA = **one concrete question** they can answer in 1–2 sentences.
- Bias toward frictions AI is often good at: high-volume unstructured
  communication (calls/texts/notes), scheduling + dispatch, parts/inventory
  chasing, paperwork / job notes, customer follow-up and rescheduling.
- Every question must **force a reasonably specific answer**: situation-bound
  (“when the board gets full…”, “when a customer call goes sideways…”) and/or
  a concrete forced choice. Options themselves should be concrete (not vague
  buckets). Prefer “getting the right tech to the right job with the right
  info” over “getting techs to the right job.”
- Reject broad asks (“what admin work do you still do?”, “where HVAC still loses
  time”) unless tightened into a forced choice or a situational frame.
- Not vague (“what do you think?”). Not a disguised meeting ask.
- If the user’s real goal is a meeting, **reframe** into an intrigue question
  and say so in one short line. Do not write the call ask.

## Batch gate (2+ emails in one go)

Hard fail and rewrite if a name/role swap would make two emails interchangeable.

Across the set you must have:

- **Different opening moves** (vary the problem-first vertical beat; never
  identical arcs). Trade batch: `Hi {First},` is shared form, not a stock phrase
  fail — vary what follows.
- **Different concrete tensions** (not the same pain rephrased per role title)
- **Zero shared stock phrases** (no repeated disclaimers, identical closers,
  or copy-pasted vertical sentences)
- Most openings still hit the sharp spine: problem-first vertical + before I
  build the wrong thing (prefer over product-first “I build software…”)
