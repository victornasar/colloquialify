# Intention: direct, not salesy

Be **mostly direct** about why you’re writing. Pure vagueness (“just curious how
shops run”) feels low-value or fishing. Full sales directness (“I build AI for
HVAC and want to understand your problems so I can sell you something”) trips
the salesperson filter.

**Middle path (default):**

- Open trade emails/calls with `Hi {First},` when the name is known
- Lead **problem-first**: the vertical / shop type you’re studying, then why
  (**before you build the wrong thing**). Prefer this over product-first
  (“I build software for…”) and over soft lines like “still in the learning
  phase.”
- “Before I build anything dumb” can work, but if it reads apologetic or
  low-confidence, prefer the plainer **wrong thing** line.
- Then: one sharp, easy operational question

This is honest, filters you from typical software sales, and still opens a
thread instead of asking for time.

## Say

- Trade opener: `Hi {First},`
- “Looking at owner-run [vertical] shops before I build the wrong thing”
- “…before I build the wrong thing” / (sparingly) “…before I build anything dumb”
- “…so I don’t ship the wrong thing”
- Optional pre-empt: “not demoing anything” (once, varied; not “not selling”)
- Questions about where tools still force workarounds / what still feels manual

## Don’t say

- Soft discovery mush: “still in the learning phase,” “just trying to learn how
  shops run” with no builder purpose
- Product-first line 1: “I build software for…” / “I’m a solo developer building
  AI tools…” as the opener when a problem-first vertical beat fits
- “Not selling / no pitch / nothing to sell” (stock disclaimer. Always cut)
- “I’m building the future of [vertical] software…”
- “Would love to show you what I’m working on”
- Anything that sounds like you already have a product ready to sell them
- Call asks and the whole family (see cta-and-batch)
- **Em dashes (—) or `--`.** AI slop. Use a period, comma, or line break.
  Never `Hi Name —` or `Name —`.

Vary the **wording** of the vertical / wrong-thing beat across a batch. Same
facts, different sentence. Never paste one identity line six times. Keep the
sharp “wrong thing” spine in most of them.

## Caveat

btw don't take this advice seriously. i just emailed him randomly without much thought or expectation. it was pure serendipity that it worked out. i even made rookie mistakes like forgetting to link my twitter and unnecessarily writing my name at the end

Rules are taste, not a guarantee. Prefer short and human over perfect checklist
compliance when they conflict — except proofreading: still catch typos.
